In function theory, **Image** and **Pre-image** describe the two directions of a mapping \\(f: X \to Y\\):

---

### **1. Image (The Output)**
* **Definition:** When an input element \\(x\\) from the domain \\(X\\) maps to an output \\(y\\) in the codomain \\(Y\\) via \\(y = f(x)\\), \\(y\\) is called the **image of \\(x\\) under \\(f\\)**.
* **Collection of Images:** The set containing all actual output values (images of \\(X\\)) is called the **range** or the **image of \\(X\\) under \\(f\\)**.

---

### **2. Pre-Image (The Input)**
* **Definition:** If \\(y = f(x)\\), the original input element \\(x\\) in domain \\(X\\) is called the **pre-image of \\(y\\)**.
* **Collection of Pre-Images:** The set of all input elements in \\(X\\) that point to a specific value \\(y \in Y\\) is called the **inverse image** (or set pre-image) of \\(y\\).

---

### **3. Simple Concrete Example**
Let domain \\(X = \{a, b, c\}\\) and codomain \\(Y = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}\\), with function \\(f\\) defined as:
* \\(f(a) = 2\\)
* \\(f(b) = 4\\)
* \\(f(c) = 2\\)

Here:
* **Image of \\(a\\):** \\(2\\) is the image of \\(a\\) (and also the image of \\(c\\)).
* **Pre-images of \\(2\\):** \\(a\\) and \\(c\\) are both pre-images of \\(2\\).
* **Pre-image of \\(4\\):** \\(b\\) is the pre-image of \\(4\\).
* **Range (Set of all Images):** \\(\{2, 4\}\\).

---

### **Quick Analogy**
Think of a function as taking a picture:
* **Object (\\(x\\)):** The person standing in front of the camera (**Pre-image**).
* **Photograph (\\(y\\)):** The resulting image on screen (**Image**).
